how the lab teaches

Three things the PPF makes visible.

If the chart above seems abstract, three observations help. Each one is a move you will return to later in the course.

1. Opportunity cost is the slope. The Opportunity Cost readout is the slope of the frontier at the production point. Move the slider toward "all computers" and watch the opportunity cost get larger. The last computers cost more bread to produce than the first ones did, because the resources being redirected are getting worse and worse at making computers.

2. Growth is the curve moving outward. Click "grow the economy 10%." The whole frontier shifts. The economy can now produce more bread for the same amount of computers (or more computers for the same amount of bread, or some of both). This is what economic growth looks like in this diagram. Unit 9 takes up what makes the frontier move.

3. Inside the curve is wasted potential. The chart doesn't let you move inside the frontier in this version of the lab; the production point sits on the curve. But it could. A recession is when an economy operates at a point inside its frontier: factories below capacity, workers unemployed, machines idle. Unit 11 takes that up.
